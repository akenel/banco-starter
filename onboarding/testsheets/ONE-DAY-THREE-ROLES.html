What this actually tests, and nothing else does: the handovers. Each role works fine alone — the failures live where one person's day becomes another's. On 2026-08-03 the shift report totalled 2 transactions while the itemised log underneath it listed 1, because a filter was fixed in one place and not in its twin.

Department keys are new and untouched by a human. Ring them like you mean it. Angel's read: "now the users can actually fall back in a way they did before — it simulates it almost exactly." Her day-book line Grips 15.– and a department line Grips 15.00 are the same information. This is the day that proves it.

Before you start — five minutes, and it saves the run

0a Sign out and back in on the tablet. Existing sessions keep the OLD 30-minute idle timeout; a fresh login gets the hour.☐
0b Hard-refresh (Ctrl+Shift+R) or use a private window, once, on each device. There is a service worker.☐
0c Have two real products that scan in your hand — papers and filters are the safest bet (55% and 9% bound).☐
0d Have one thing with no barcode — a bong or a grinder off the shelf. This is the whole point.☐

PAM sells the things that cannot scan

#Do thisExpectP / FWhat happened
3Scan the bong / grinder that has no barcode. A calm amber banner, not a pop-up: “Nicht gefunden — Abteilung wählen”. Nothing to dismiss. The scan box keeps the cursor.☐
4Tap Glas (or Grips). Type the price. Add. Price field is its own full-width line with a label. “✓ No name needed.” Goes in the cart as Glas.☐
5🎯 MIXED CART. In the SAME sale, also scan a real product. Take cash. Both lines on one ticket. 5-rappen rounding applies to the tender, not to the department line. Nothing else on the day has exercised this.☐
6Ring Grips twice more at different prices (say 9.– and 35.–). A real day repeats a bucket. This is what makes the close block worth reading.☐
7⚠️ Type 250.00 against Glas and press Add. The button turns red and says Confirm. It does NOT ring on the first press. Press again → it rings. (Never pressed by a human before.)☐
8Try 1500.00 against any button. Refused — above the 999.00 ceiling, with a message telling you to use the catalogue.☐
9🧪 THE SILENT DROP. Scan an unknown barcode, then instead of picking a department, scan a real product. The banner just goes away. No nag, no error, no leftover code attached to the next line. Designed behaviour (§4.2) that nobody has verified.☐
10Pick a department, then tap “…or create a real product instead”. The full create form comes back and keeps the price you typed. Pressing ✕ instead should NOT keep it.☐
⏱️ Time step 4 against step 2. How many seconds does a department sale take versus a scan? Write it down — doc 10 says two seconds or it is broken, and if the fallback is slower than paper nobody will use it.   scan: ________ s    department: ________ s

RALPH works and closes the day — manager

#Do thisExpectP / FWhat happened
14Ring one more sale as Ralph — one department line, one scan. Same behaviour as Pam. A manager should not get a different till.☐
15🎯 Open Reports. Find the 🏷️ Departments block. All ten buttons listed in strip order, including the ones that took nothing (greyed). Glas and Grips carry today's money.☐

FELIX reads it the next morning — owner, was not there

This is the only part I cannot judge at all. Everything above is a fact that can be checked. This is a question about whether the numbers mean anything to the person who pays for them — and Felix is the only one who can answer it.

#Ask FelixHis answer, in his words
22Open the dashboard / reports for yesterday. Without any explanation from you — what does he think the Departments block is?
23“Does Glas 95.–, Grips 44.– tell you anything useful, or do you need to know which bong?”
24“If a third of your sales looked like this every day, is that a problem or is that fine?”
25“Would you rather the cashier spent 30 seconds finding the real product, or 5 seconds pressing Glas?”
26Show him the “Rung from the catalogue %”. “Is that a number you would look at every week?”
Why ask 25 at all. The whole design bets that a fast bucket beats a slow lookup. If Felix says he would rather have the 30 seconds, the bet is wrong for his shop and it is much cheaper to hear that now than after a month of data.
