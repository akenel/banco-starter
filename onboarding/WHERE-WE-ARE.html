The POS window walks off the right of the screen
Layla, 4 Sep 21:29, mid-sheet: a strip of the till and a field of desktop wallpaper. "i will reboot to resolve this issue — only thing a cashier could do — i see it happen earlier today." She was right, and nothing in Banco can prevent it: Chromium is running in a normal window with a title bar, so a stray touch drags it away and a touchscreen gives you no easy way to drag it back. The fix is one flag on the tablet — launch Chromium with --kiosk (or at minimum --start-fullscreen --window-position=0,0) in its autostart entry. No title bar, no drag, nothing to walk off. Belongs with the counter visit below — same trip, ten minutes.

6 · What "ready" means

Section 1 clear, section 2 at least looked at, and section 3 tested by a person. Nothing in sections 4 or 5 stops a cashier ringing a sale.
The rule Layla gave us today, now in the codebase. "Having the warning above the input is a good idea for all warnings and saves us a lot of time fooling around." On a touch till the keypad owns the bottom of the screen, so below a field is structurally the wrong place for anything that has to be read. Five inline warnings sat under keypad-wired fields; three moved — both discount refusals on the cart and the price refusal on shelf intake. Two stayed on purpose: a quota note and a green "applied" confirmation. A confirmation you miss costs nothing; a refusal you miss is a customer charged a price nobody agreed to.
One thing that is on no list. Everything this week was proved in the flat. The shop's network, the shop's lighting and the shop's counter have not been in the loop since the tablet was set up — and that exact gap has bitten before, when LTE was proved at home on an SSID Luzern does not have. An hour at that counter before the shift is worth more than another day of fixes from here.

And it now has a second job. The --kiosk flag in section 1 is a ten-minute change on that tablet, and until it is done "reboot" really is a cashier's only move when the window walks off the screen. Two problems, one trip.
