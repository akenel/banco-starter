# 15 · The doorway script — fifteen minutes with a busy owner

*Written 2026-08-05 for a real one: Felix at the door of Artemis at 07:34, unloading stock, fifteen
minutes. The Artemis specifics are the example; the shape is reusable, because **every shop owner
you will ever demo to is standing up, holding something, and half-listening.***

> **The rule the whole thing hangs on:** a shop owner's fifteen minutes are worth more than your
> update. Spend them on **what only he can answer**, not on what you did.

---

## −1 min · Prep, before you walk in

**Switch the label printer on now.** The first job after a wake takes **25–30 seconds** for roll
calibration. Fumbling with a dead printer while he stands there burns a fifth of the meeting.

- Printer on, lit, green
- Tablet awake, Banco open in kiosk
- Gun paired and tested on one scan

You want to be scanning within ten seconds of saying hello.

---

## 0–3 min · Don't explain. Hand it to him.

He is holding stock. **Scan something in his hands, print its label, put the label in his hand.**

No words are needed for the part that matters — he sees a tablet with no cable to anything produce a
price label. One sentence while it prints:

> *"That's the tablet you gave me to scrap. It's a till now. Cost nothing."*

That is the entire status update. Everything else is detail he did not ask for.

---

## 3–8 min · The question only he can answer — while he is standing in stock

For Artemis, 2026-08-05, that is **the Cannazym**: two rows, same name, **CHF 43.90** and
**CHF 21.00**. One has a real EAN; the other has no barcode at all, so nothing in the database can
say what it physically is. **Only the bottle knows.**

> *"Have you got two different Cannazym? I've got it at 43.90 and at 21.00 and I can't tell if
> that's two products or one mistake."*

He is unloading stock. This is the best five minutes of his week to ask it, and one answer unblocks
the merge screen and the ~40 duplicates behind it.

**The general form:** bring the *one* judgement that is blocked on him being physically present.
Not a status, not a list — the thing where the shop floor is the only source of truth.

---

## 8–11 min · Two questions, thirty seconds each

Short, factual, instantly answerable. For Artemis:

1. **Which paid-out reasons do you actually use?** Safe, supplier, petty cash, wages? Closes an open
   testsheet item in one breath.
2. Anything else that is a *fact he holds*, not a decision he must weigh.

---

## 11–15 min · What happens today, so nothing surprises him

> *"I'm going through the shelves with the scanner today, section at a time. Nothing changes for
> you, nothing goes live."*

---

## If he only has two minutes

Print the label. Ask the one question. Leave.

---

## ⛔ What you do not say

**Debian. Bluetooth. CUPS. Failover. Hotspots. On-prem. The offline kit.**

None of it is his problem, and every sentence costs you a question you actually needed answered. He
wants to know two things — *does it work* and *will it slow my staff down* — and the label in his
hand already answered both.

**Save the real decisions for when he is sitting down.** Neither of these survives a doorway:

- The realm passwords, before the shop trades on it
- Reading the German cash-box screens as a shopkeeper, not a developer

---

## Why this is in the onboarding kit at all

Because the failure it prevents is not technical. Doc 10's line — *"Setup is not selling"* — has a
sibling: **a demo is not a status meeting.** Turn up with a list and you will leave with the list
still in your hand and nothing decided. Turn up with a label and one question and you leave with an
answer that unblocks forty rows.
